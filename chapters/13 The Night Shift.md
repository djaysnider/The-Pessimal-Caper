# The Night Shift

“Eugene!?!  Has anyone seen Eugene?” Carson was pacing the floor.

“He’s ... uhhhh ... sick,” Reggie said running across the lobby to the front desk. “He asked me to cover his shift for him.”

“Now, Reggie, I hardly think that’s a good idea. We need you in top form tomorrow for the debutantes and their families.”

“It’s fine, Mr. Carson, I can get a couple of naps in during my breaks.”

“Well, I don’t like it, but it doesn’t seem I have any choice.  Fine, we’ve got another late check-in.  Get these bags up to 1225.” He gestured to two large suitcases and a hatbox already on the cart.

“1225, yessir.  I’m on it,” Reggie said as enthusiastically as he could while pushing the cart to the service elevator.

He got no further than thirty feet before an elderly man with a very familiar wooden case came out of the lounge. “Oh, my boy!  I’m delighted I’ve found you again. I need a favor.”

Reggie stopped.  “Certainly, Mr. Burkman. What can I do for you?”

“Well, I’ve got a lovely fan ... er lady companion, I suppose you could say ... who desperately wants to have a few drinks and dances this evening.  I was wondering if you might take Dr. Whispers here upstairs to the room and put him to bed.” He smiled and winked mischievously as held out two quarters between index and ring finger.

“Certainly, Mr. Burkman,” Reggie said, accepting the coins and lifting his hand in respect. Room 1517, right?”

“Right you are, my boy.”

Reggie accepted the case, loaded it onto the front of his cart, and continued towards the elevator.

He was shocked to find me there, arm around a clarinet player, who was trying to get from the loading dock to the elevator just as the kitchen manager, Mr. Jenkins, showed up looking for Pete the dishwasher. Still trying to keep the Zombie infestation on the downlow, I quickly steered the clarinet player back outside before Jenkins caught on. Once that was accomplished, I only had to talk fast enough to keep him confused and get Reg caught up.

“Sorry, you know how absent-minded woodwind players can be.  Anyway, no, Mr. Jenkins, I haven’t seen anyone who meets that description who answers to the name of Pete, nor a housekeeper named Gilda, nor a line cook named Henry.  It’s very odd, and I’m sorry I’m on a case right now, or I’d be happy to help you find them, but I tell you what, you’re probably quite busy. Let me find Mr. Pierce and put him on it for you. Don’t bother yourself with a thing, just stay back there in your little kitchen area.”

Pierce was another concern I hadn’t taken into account.  He'd likely wander the halls looking for me once he gave up trying to impress our Miss Halston-Kensington. I needed a distraction to keep him occupied for at least a few hours.  Jenkins was less of a problem compared to that.  All he wanted was to finish his dishes, collect his tablecloths, and go home. I’d already spotted that quick fix.

“In fact, here’s Reggie O’Grady, you know Reggie, right? He’s filling in for Eugene, who’s home sick ... Tuberculosis, I think ... definitely going to be out for a while. Anyway, Reg is a very stand-up guy who knows how to get things done around here.  I bet he’d be happy to drop by the kitchen and help out as soon as he drops those bags off wherever they go.  Wouldn’t ya, Reg?”

“I suppose that would be sufficient, thank you, Mr. O’Grady.  I won’t forget this.”

Reggie doffed his cap again as Jenkins started back to the kitchen, double-time.

“Mr. Chase, I’m already covering for Eugene, I don’t have time to---“

“You heard him. He won’t forget it.  This could be your big ticket out of the bellboy business.”

“And into the busboy business?  I don’t think so.”

“Look, kid, we’re all doing a lot of unpleasant things right now, but the fewer middle and upper management types in this hotel tonight the better, so let’s just put everyone at ease and try to get them to go home.”

It was at this point the clarinet player came back, looking no less confused than before, still trying to get back to the elevator and practice.

“Sorry, pal, I just couldn’t have Jenkins looking at you in that state.  People talk.  You understand, right?” Yes, I realize I had started talking to zombies, even though they themselves didn’t seem to notice.  This one just continued shuffling toward the elevator.

That is, until he spotted Reggie, at which point his eyes went wider, he double his pace, and took the widest possible arc around Reggie, never taking his eyes off him, until he was safely in the elevator and the doors were closing.

“See, that guy runs on pure instinct, and even he’s starting to see a real change in you.  I’m tellin’ ya, by this time tomorrow, everyone here’s gonna owe you a favor.  You’ll probably be running the place.”

Reggie scowled and nodded.  “Yeah, everyone’s gonna owe me a favor. Not the least of which is you.  I’m thinking my stake in your $200 bonus just went up to thirty bucks.”

“Seriously? Kid? I’m down here manhandling zombies trying to save this hotel’s reputation.  You’re just doing a few dishes.  That job doesn’t pay $15 a week.”

“Oh, that’s fine Mr. Chase.  I can just head home.  I need to feed my cat anyway.”

“Okay, fine $30 ... IF we get the jewels back and the ball goes off without a hitch.  Now, just do your part and hurry back.”

The elevator dinged and out stepped Sally Montana.  I risked a look inside the car. She’d managed to travel alone.  This was becoming a less-and-less frequent occurrence. “Oh, Mr. Chase, I’m so glad I found you. Listen. I’ve worked it out. All we need is the number of the suite the Upper Weaver girls have rented for tomorrow. It should be easy for Reggie here to look up. It was purchased with one of Mr. Moray’s credit cards. When we have that, I can sneak in and hide somewhere with the fake necklace, Reggie makes a distraction at the door, when they go to investigate, I swap the fake for the real one.  Then you can nab the thief, and we can publicly expose the girls when they present the fake one as part of whatever their plan was.

“Kid that’s a ridiculous plan.  Why don’t we just confirm the jewel thief has the goods on him, nab him red handed, and let him give up the girls?”

“Oh, just let me have my moment, and we’ll add a scene between Chapter 11 and 12 to get it all to make more sense.  The point is, my plan gets you your $200 bonus.”

“I can get you that room number, Sally,” Reggie said, dribbling all over her saddle shoes like a lovesick puppy.  “Unfortunately, in addition to my regular duties, I’m doing KP tonight, thanks to Mr. Chase here.”

Before I could protest, the wheels in Sally’s head were already clicking away. “Well, I can take these up for you.  That should give you time to look up the suite number and do the rest of that.”

“Aww, geez, you’d do that for me?”

“Thinking nothing of it.  Where do they go.”

“Well, the suitcases and the hat book go to 1225.  The case is Mr. Berkman’s, and it goes to 1517.”

I instantly recoiled two paces back from the front of that cart.  “Jeez, kid, you had that cursed vessel here the whole time and you didn’t warn me?! That’s bad form!”

“Cursed vessel?” Sally asked.

“Mr. Chase is afraid of ventriloquist dummies,” Reggie explained.

“Not afraid, kid, just apparently more aware than you are of how dangerous those things are when left unattended!”

There was a moan of disgust from the doorway to the loading dock.  I turned to see a tall figure with hollow eyes, sunken cheeks, and one arm missing below the elbow.  To his credit, he was still wearing most of a bellboy’s uniform and hat.

“Eugene!” Reggie ghasped.

“Eugene?!” Sally exclaimed.  “He looks like ... like ...”

I attempted to be the voice of reason. “Yes, fine, the hotel is in the middle of a small ... zombie infestation.  It’s nothing to be concerned about.  For the most part, they only hang out in the ballroom and the loading dock.”

“Ballroom?! We’ve been up there most of the day!”

“Exactly why you should believe me when I say this is a small problem and not one you need to worry -- or tell anyone -- about.”

“How can I not---“

“And in exchange for your cooperation, I will go along with your unnecessarily elaborate plan involving the Upper Weaver girls and the necklace, okay?  Happy?  Now, you go deliver Reggie’s luggage for him.  Reggie, you get that suite number for her.  I’m going to try to get this big fella downstairs to the storage room with the others.”

“How many others are there??!”

“Look, we can discuss all this later.  Right now, I need this elevator to get him down to the storage room.  You can take one of the main elevators in the lobby.”

Sally did as she was told, and she and Reggie backed out to the main lobby.  I pressed the call button and tried to make small talk with Eugene as we waited for the elevator.  Eugene wasn’t having any of it though, and was very insistent to following Sally and Reggie to the lobby.  Despite being considerably bigger than him and having both arms and working eyes, he was surprisingly tough and kept pressing me back.  This was no good.  Obviously, if he made it to the lobby and was spotted, we could kiss all of our plans goodbye.

“Come on, big guy, why don’t we go have a smoke or something.  You don’t want to go in there, they’ll put you to work.”

There was the echo of an inhuman, gutteral, half sane laughter that seemed to come from everywhere. I don’t mind telling you it set my teeth on edge.  Then came the voice.

“Who knows what’s on the minds of mindless men? The Apparition is certain.  Muahahahahahah!”

“What?!” I asked to no one in particular.

There was a quiet popping sound, and Eugene suddenly started cowering by the elevator door.  When it dinged, I heard two more similar pops, and Eugene had moved to the back of the car and gone fetal. I wasted no time rushing over, pressing the button for the basement and standing back as the door closed.

I turned to find the source of whatever had driven him back and saw him for the first time -- black trenchcoat with upturned collar, wide-brimmed black slouch hat, and a bright red domino mask.  He held two lengths of pipe in each hand which he twirled with a flourish and attached to hooks inside his coat.

“What the hell are you supposed to be?”

“I,” he said, bowing with a flourish, “am The Apparition, at your service.”
