# The Gunsel

The dinner rush had subsided.  The parents were doing the social thing in the lounge waiting for Doctor Creepy and his incredible trained ventriloquist to do their shtick.  I wasn't about to have any part of that mess.  Reggie was back at his post, waiting for Eugene to come relieve him, still fawning over that Montana chick as if a future high society dame like that was going to drop everything and run away with a bellboy.  I tried to tell him I stood a better chance with an opportunistic social climber like Peyton Ashton-Hamiton than he had with second generation money like that.  You can't talk to kids these days though.

The second shift at the hotel is pretty routine.  Weatherby usually stuck around for a few hours until arriving guests were settled in and settled in guests were sufficiently liquored up.  That gave the staff a few hours of peace until Carson came in for the night shift, and the hotel went into “preparation" mode for the next morning's flurry of breakfast rush, checkouts, cleaning, and check-ins.  This was the quiet; the calm before an inevitable storm.

A lot of things bothered me about this case. It should have been simple “guard the  jewels" money.  Instead, it had turned into a recovery and concealment job involving international gangsters, old money, the undead, and whatever that talking hunk of wood had up its tiny sleeves.

What bothered me more was Meter Malone's sudden disappearance. I could really use another capable hand now that zombies were involved. Maybe Meter had heard through his grapevine that the dingus had been nabbed already and thought that gave him the night off, but I know he would have checked with me first before giving up a sawbuck to nap in a fancy hotel lobby.

Until he showed, there was nothing to do but assume his post.  I took a seat in a wingback chair near the main elevators, used a particuarly obstinante fern for cover, and scanned the lobby. It was mostly empty aside from staff.  Reggie popped in and out with a rolling cart full of luggage every half hour or so.  Every once in a while, down the service corridor, I heard the old freight elevator ding and saw a shadowy figure move toward the loading dock. It was odd how they never any interest whatsoever in the lobby.  Well, maybe not. I still heard the sporadic laughter from the city's upper crust in the lounge as Berkman insulted each and every one of them personally, then blamed Doctor Whispers for his seemingly unreal autonomy, as if it wasn't actually happening in front of his eyes.

“Lookin' for your friend, Chase?”  came a familiar voice from directly behind me.

I turned and saw an entire tough-guy routine in minature form.  He was maybe five foot three, wore a baggy gray trench coat with arms so long they covered his hands, and a black fedora two sizes too big.  He looked like a kid playing dressup.  Well, a kid with gold teeth and an inkvine scar bisecting the right side of his face.

“I’m “I don't have any friends, Gunsel.”

“Sorry, looking for your cabbie, Chase?”

That triggered something inside me. Meter was no great thinker, but he was wise. He wasn't the type to get in a dangerous situation with an obvious tough guy, or in this case, wannabe tough guy.  I slowly stood up, turned around, and planted myself inches from his more vital organs.  “You implying something, Gunsel?”

The gunsel looked up at me, towering easily a foot above him.  To his credit, he didn't flinch.  He smiled and cocked his head to one side. “I'm tellin' ya. He ain't comin’.”

“Oh, yeah? I suppose you had something to do with that?”

“Well, he's your friend, but he's easily bought, Chase.”

“Sounds like I didn’t need him anyway ... Penny.”

His face went white.  I knew I had him.

“Don't start, Chase.”

“Noooooo, Pendragon Mészáros, the Hungarian butcher’s kid.”

“It's Nickles O'Malley now,” he said in a way that I knew was going to be trouble in two chapters.

“Yeah, I should have known.  They said some guy from Cleveland was hanging out playing tough guy with a violin case. Oh, maybe now, but I knew you when your father used to give me free bologna for chasing cats out of his store.”

“Whatever deal you had with my father doesn't apply to you and me, Chase.”

“No, you're right, you were just that scared kid, afraid of rats, hanging out in the stockroom surrounded by comforting salamis, practicing that violin of yours.  Oh, it’s all comin’ together now.”

His hand went to his pocket as tears welled up in his eyes. “Keep pushin' me, Chase. One day, you're gonna get yours.”

“I’m not scared, Penny, and that’s the point. On the other hand, if anything bad ever happened to my dear friend Meter Malone?”

Penny shrugged. “We all gotta go sometime, Chase.”

“Now listen to me, tough guy, if Meter Malone should die any way other than fat and happy, or from a painless aneurysm while making love to his mistress?”

“Those are oddly specific---”

I stepped in and got right in Penny’s face. “But, especially if he should meet an unpleasant end at the hand of some two-bit gunsel with daddy issues and an axe to grind---”

“What? No, I didn’t kill him! Geez, Chase, he was a good cabbie. You know how hard those are to come by?  Private dicks, on the other hand?  Well, some lives are cheap.”

“Meter did see something, didn’t he? He just didn’t know he’d seen something. That’s why they sent you.  You’re supposed to get us out of the picture.  So, I was on to something after all. Somebody’s getting nervous. The jewels are gone. I thought I blew the case, but they still threw a saw buck to the shortest, least intimidating tough guy in the city to try to scare me off. Maybe the Montana girl is on to something after all. Am I getting warm yet, Penny?”

“It’s Nickels!”

“Not since the crash, Mészáros.”

“Keep talkin', tough guy.”

“Oh, I'm not that tough. If I were, they’d have spent more money on the intimidation. I mean, come on, I get bigger threats for free with my corn flakes.”

“You got a piece, big guy?  Let's go.”

“Penny, we're in a hotel lobby.  The hotel detective, Pierce, hates my guts more than you do, but even he's going to gun you down the minute he spots a bulge in your coat pocket while my hands are sitting helplessly at my sides.”

“Pierce ain’t here though, is he? It's almost third shift.  Anything goes with the third shift. I'm sure they've seen worse.”

My brain was thinking twice as fast as it should have by this point. “So, is that it? Pierce hired you?  Nah, it can't be Pierce, he wouldn't resist a chance to go up against me. Somebody’s holding his leash. He's a former cop, so he knows all the up-and-coming two-bit gunmen, and you're just a Penny pretending to be a nickel.”

“Keep talkin', Chase.”  The kid was practically in tears now.

“Don’t you get it, Penny? They set you up to fail the same way they did me. Somebody sent you in here, knowing I’d spot you and probably just break your legs or something rather than ruin the carpets. Then Weatherby, has no choice but to let Pierce, the real tough guy, come take me down. So, really, you’ve done your job, and you’ve done it admirably, so just go home, call him and tell him I broke your legs, and we’re all a lot happier in the morning.”

“That’s it,” Penny hissed, his hand shifted in his pocket, and I saw the distinct ouline of a snub nosed revolver. “Step outside.”

“What is that?  Oh, come on, is that an old Colt Detective Special?” I shook my head. “I am so tired of unregistered thugs who refuse to honor the intent of the branding. You couldn’t detect your way out of a paper bag, yet you picked that over the far superior police special?! I’m actually a bit insulted on behalf of detectives everywhere.”

“Move it, Chase. Outside.”

“No, Penny, and for two very important reasons. First off, I’d rather be shot by your silly little snub-nosed.38...” I pulled back my coat revealing my piece, sitting calmly in its shoulder holster, “...than my .45 ACP any day of the week.”

Penny went white, and the bulge in his pocket disappeared. “And second?” He said, sounding afraid of the answer.

“I promised my lady dinner, and she’d hate it if I stood her up.”

On cue, Peyton Halston-Kensington rested the blunt end of a fountain pen against Penny’s right kidney, leaned in, and whispered into his ear, “My coat’s expensive. I’d hate to put a hole in it, but I will.”  

I snapped my fingers towards the bellhop desk.  Reggie immediately sped to my side. “This is Reggie the Knows,” I said, instantly regretting the homonym.  “Reg, this is Penny. Penny is an annoying but potentially dangerous hitman. During the day, he’s also heir to the Mészáros meat empire.”

Reggie smiled eagerly. “Oh, sure, I love Mészáros. Very nice to meet you, Penny--”

“Alright, zip it, kid. You’re missing the point. If Penny here isn’t gone in the next 45 seconds, or if you see his face in this hotel lobby again, I want you to contact the heads of the families up in the ballroom, and put the word out on Mr. Mészáros here.”

Penny smirked. “Families?! Those aren’t crime families, Chase. Exactly what word do you think you’re putting out?”

“The word that Mészáros Deli has offered to cater the entire banquet tomorrow night at ... what’s a good rate? A 60% discount? No, let’s be generous. There’s a lot of opportunity for exposure here.  Make it a 75% discount.”

Penny went white. “75%, and less than 24 hours’ notice?! My dad will kill me!”

“Oh, but 24 of the wealthiest, most influential families in the city would be so grateful. On the other hand, if you didn’t deliver, then you would have stiffed exactly the type of disgruntled customers businesses don’t recover from.  Get me?”  I looked at my watch dramatically. “Now. We were talking about 45 seconds.”

Penny turned and sprinted for the revolving door.
