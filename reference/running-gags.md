# Running-Gags

## 1. Necklace mystery: three distinct layers

Sally's late mother's necklace is a cherished family heirloom reputed to be cursed. Peyton claims she wants to protect it, but wants to steal it. Jack suggests substituting a convincing duplicate. Peyton engages Luk. The swap has occurred by the end of Chapter 7, when Jack examines the necklace with Charles and discovers a fake.

The **real necklace** protects the wearer and people within roughly a **ten-foot radius** from the scientist's basement-generated energy field, which would otherwise turn exposed people into zombies. Existing zombies recoil or flee from the protected zone. Luk's unwitting possession makes him a walking refuge, repeatedly misunderstood by the others.

Peyton has targeted the **less valuable of two similarly named, similarly appearing historic necklaces**. The valuable one went to Charles's great-great-aunt; the consolation necklace went to her sister, Charles's great-great-grandmother. Charles knows this, and Peyton does not.

**TENTATIVE / UNRESOLVED:**

- Whether the necklace's protection is truly supernatural or an accidental physical/material interference with the energy field.
- Whether the protection can cure zombies or only prevent transformation and repel them; current description supports prevention/repulsion, **not a confirmed cure**.
- How the historical reputation for being “cursed” relates to its actual protective property.
- Whether Peyton knows about the protective effect.
- Exact theft logistics, custody chain and time between Chapter 4 and Chapter 7.
- How the real necklace returns to its rightful owner.

The supposedly inferior necklace may be the only genuinely extraordinary one; the historically valuable necklace could be ordinary jewelry. This fits the established protection mechanism but was not separately confirmed as an intended thematic statement.

## 2. Zombie / orchestra / scientist subplot

The mad scientist's basement experiment generates an energy field that converts unprotected people into zombies. The ballroom musicians have already become zombies, but continue playing with such professional dedication that they have not stopped to eat brains. They have been playing **three days without a break**. Their conductor is still alive. He refuses Wetherby's requests for access to the ballroom, claiming rehearsals cannot be interrupted. Sally's friends mistakenly believe the music indicates rehearsals for their surprise singer.

Zombies are afraid of Luk's proximity because he is carrying the protective necklace. Characters try explanations involving his accessories or personal traits; those hypotheses fail when they separate from him.

Scientist's actual purpose, why the field affects people this way, the exact onset and progression of the outbreak, how Bellamy survives, the band's condition at the finale, and whether anyone can reverse zombification. Do not invent explanations merely to close these gaps.
