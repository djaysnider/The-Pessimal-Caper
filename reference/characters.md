# Characters and Relationships

## Characters and relationships

### Jack Chase

**CONFIRMED** — First-person narrator and middle-aged hard-boiled private detective: cynical, too earnest, inexplicably well informed about monsters, curses and bizarre prior cases. He takes insane situations professionally and seriously; he is **not** meta-aware that he's in a detective parody. He has elaborate, opinionated digressions about the conventions and irritations of his trade. He particularly despises cursed jewelry and forged-duplicate MacGuffin cases. He once encountered **“The Mystery of the Haunted Delicatessen”**; this should probably remain unexplained. He prefers straightforward zombies to Japanese gangsters whose elaborate apologetic rituals and bad haiku delay killing; he can even criticize the syllable count. Luk See knows him from an earlier impossible adventure and corroborates at least some of his claims.

**CONFIRMED** — Peyton hires Jack ostensibly to prevent a theft; Jack unwittingly proposes the substitution-with-forgery method she will arrange to use. Jack later rails against precisely this sort of crime. His narration is retrospective, from roughly two years after the Beauchamp events, and can reconstruct scenes he did not personally witness. He may be telling the entire story to someone on his next case.

### Reggie O'Grady

**CONFIRMED** — Young, idealistic bellhop; clumsy and incompetent at ordinary bellhop tasks but observant, resourceful, always nearby, and able to supply just the right tool or information. Hotel connective tissue and reader proxy. Unlike Jack, he reacts with appropriate alarm to zombies and gangsters; he may understand how to avoid zombies before Jack does.

### Sally Montana

**CONFIRMED** — Clever young debutante celebrating coming of age, about to wear a necklace inherited from her deceased mother. She suspects her fellow debutantes of conspiring to steal it. They **are** secretly conspiring, but to arrange a surprise performance by a young celebrity singer, not to steal her jewelry. The girls think the necklace is gaudy. Once Sally discovers the necklace is a forgery, she **pretends it's genuine**, hoping the thieves will believe the original was fake too. The girls listen at the ballroom door, believing the singer is rehearsing with the orchestra, without knowing the musicians are zombies.

**TENTATIVE** — Singer might be **Dennis Day** (a strong period possibility) or, depending on chronology, **Frank Sinatra**. Neither is selected.

### Charles Montana

**CONFIRMED** — Sally's father; rich, successful, genuinely kind, affectionate and trusting. Widower of Peyton's sister. Delighted Sally has reached the age to wear her mother's necklace. Mr. Magoo-like comic archetype, not secretly cunning or stupid: loses his glasses early, mistakes zombies and masked vigilantes for ordinary hotel guests, and thinks the disastrous ball a magnificent success. His sincere affection for his daughter and late wife grounds the comedy.

**CONFIRMED — Late payoff:** Charles learns Peyton stole the necklace because of its famous name and supposed provenance, then casually explains she has the **wrong necklace**. The famous necklace belonged to **his great-great-aunt**; the similarly named, similarly looking, much less valuable necklace Sally inherited was given as a consolation to **his great-great-grandmother**, the famous owner's sister. Charles knows the family story Peyton has misunderstood. This is not a secret-mastermind reveal.

**TENTATIVE** — The exact moment Charles learns of Peyton's involvement, his response, and whether he has known of her wrongdoing earlier are undecided.

### Peyton Halston-Kensington

**CONFIRMED** — Wealthy, greedy socialite. **Sister of Sally's deceased mother**, hence Sally's maternal aunt and Charles's sister-in-law. She visits Jack in Chapter 1 and hires him ostensibly to discover how someone might steal Sally's family necklace during the debutante ball; in fact she intends to steal it. Jack proposes swapping it for a forgery. Peyton hires Luk See to carry out the theft and insists he hold the genuine necklace through most of Act II, so she can disown him if the forgery is noticed before the ball ends. She knows the necklace's purported history and believes it exceptionally valuable, but confuses two similar family heirlooms. Her theft is ultimately for a far less valuable piece.

**UNRESOLVED** — Whether Peyton knows the necklace's actual supernatural protection, or believes only the supposed curse and historical value.

### Luk See

**CONFIRMED** — Hitman/member of a mysterious Japanese criminal syndicate, at the hotel for a gathering of underworld families. The author prefers not to call the organization **“Yakuza,”** particularly given the period. Luk and Jack have weary professional recognition from a previous bizarre case. Luk steals the necklace and retains it for much of Act II. Zombies flee from his proximity; nobody, including Luk, initially understands that the **necklace** causes this. Characters repeatedly attribute the effect to his hat, umbrella, accent or other red herrings. He may become a reluctant ally.

**TENTATIVE** — Exact organization, Luk's other agenda, details of the past encounter, his eventual loyalty and Peyton's betrayal are not yet fixed. The name **Luk See** is the author's choice; do not silently change it.

### Eugene / Gene Pritchard

**CONFIRMED IN ACT I OUTLINE** — Reggie's fellow bellhop and foil; a brown-nosing Eddie Haskell type who flatters hotel management and undermines Reggie. The author's Chapter 2 calls him **“Eugene.”**

**TENTATIVE** — **Gene Pritchard** was the assistant's suggested full name. Whether Eugene Pritchard is final is not settled.

### Wetherby / Whetherby, hotel manager

**CONFIRMED IN ACT I OUTLINE** — Reputation-obsessed manager who wants the ballroom back in time to set up the ball and clashes with Maestro Bellamy. He tries to preserve hotel appearances amid catastrophe.

**TENTATIVE** — Assistant suggested **Cornelius P. Wetherby**. The author's Chapter 5 spells the name **“Whetherby”**; spelling remains to be standardized by the author, not silently corrected.

### Maestro Bellamy

**CONFIRMED IN ACT I OUTLINE** — Temperamental conductor, evasive about letting the manager into the ballroom, insisting that the band must continue rehearsing and that his art takes precedence over setting tables. **Not yet a zombie**, though his musicians are. He may be hiding the situation for professional pride.

**TENTATIVE** — Assistant suggested full name **Victor Bellamy**. Exact reason for his evasiveness and his knowledge of the zombie condition are undecided.

### “Slugger”

**CONFIRMED IN ACT I OUTLINE** — Famous major-league baseball player guest, introduced alongside Luk in Chapter 4; overhears Jack's speculation about how to steal the necklace and becomes a plausible suspect.

**TENTATIVE** — **“Slugger” McGraw** was the assistant's suggested name. Possible gambling connections are unconfirmed.
